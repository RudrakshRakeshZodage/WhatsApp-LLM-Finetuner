In fact, doing this is what separates an average hackathon project from a winning one.Satellites use Thermal Infrared (TIR) sensors to measure heat. TIR cannot penetrate clouds. If Mumbai is covered in monsoon clouds, the satellite reads the freezing temperature of the cloud tops, completely blinding you to the urban heat island happening on the ground below.To solve this "cloud-obscured" problem, you use Data Fusion:The AI Approach: You build a machine learning model (like Random Forest or a Spatial Interpolation neural network). You train it using historical days when the sky was clear.The Inputs: You feed the AI the real-time ground IoT data (from local weather stations), elevation data, and built-up index data (NDBI).The Output: The AI predicts and "fills in" the missing thermal pixels beneath the cloud cover by correlating the ground station data with the surrounding clear-sky pixels.

2. If you mean: "Should we combine Ground Truth data with Satellite data in general?"A massive yes. Relying strictly on satellites for PS1 is actually a trap because of the physics involved.Satellites measure $LST$ (Land Surface Temperature): This is how hot the physical concrete, asphalt, or rooftop is.
